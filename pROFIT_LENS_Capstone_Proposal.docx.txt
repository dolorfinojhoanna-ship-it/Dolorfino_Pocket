CHAPTER 1


INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
The Profit Lens platform was developed to address the need for quick, precise, and simplified profit tracking in the auto parts sales and services industry. Manual recording of sales, expenses, and inventory, especially for products such as oils, bearings, and filters is commonly observed to produce calculation errors and inconsistent financial summaries. Business owners and staff require an automated, centralized system that can provide real-time financial visibility and proactive overspending alerts.
The study is grounded in the operations of a local auto parts store owned by French Joy Salvador in Cebu City. Like many similar small businesses, the store relies on manual profit tracking, leading to time-consuming processes, missed expenses, and unclear financial reports. Profit Lens was built to replace this manual approach with a modern, reliable, web-based platform that provides automatic insights and preventive budget alerts helping owners make data-driven decisions quickly and confidently.




1.2 Statement of the Problem


The following specific problems with the current manual setup are addressed by this study:
1. Profit Monitoring Difficulty: Manually calculating monthly profits is error-prone and time-consuming, with no system to automatically compute net profit from sales and expenses.
2. Lack of Financial Reports: There is no established method for producing monthly, quarterly, or annual financial summaries. The current system has no Export or print function.
3. No Trend Analysis: Revenue reports are manually reviewed, making it impossible to visualize performance trends or compare results across months and years.
4. Unnoticed Overspending: Without automated monitoring, expenses frequently exceed the available budget without the owner's awareness.








1.3 Objectives of the Study
1.3.1 General Objective
To design and develop Profit Lens a smart, web-based profit monitoring platform that delivers automated business insights, accurate financial tracking, and proactive alerts for overspending in an auto parts sales and services environment.




1.3.2 Specific Objectives


Specifically, the study aims to:
1. To provide a module for tracking monthly profit by recording sales transactions against product costs and operating expenses.
2. To implement a financial reporting module that displays monthly, quarterly, and annual profit-and-loss summaries viewable within the system.
3. To implement automated revenue insights that display bar graphs comparing monthly revenue versus expenses across the full calendar year (January–December).
4. To develop an expense tracking module with category-based breakdowns and visual indicators that highlight high-spend areas relative to revenue.
1.4 Scope and Delimitation
Profit Lens covers the following functional areas: user authentication (admin login/register), product management, sales recording, expense tracking, revenue analysis, profit-and-loss reporting, and a financial dashboard with charts. The system is built for the auto parts sales and services industry and is designed for use by the business owner and admin-level staff.
The system does not cover: point-of-sale hardware integration, payroll processing, tax filing, external accounting system integration (e.g., QuickBooks), multi-branch management, or customer relationship management (CRM). Inventory is tracked at the product level through stock quantities updated during sales recording, but a full warehouse management system is outside scope.
1.5 Significance of the Study


* Business Owners: Provides real-time profit visibility, categorized expense breakdowns, and a full Jan–Dec revenue vs. expense bar chart, enabling faster and more confident financial decisions.


* Staff: Minimizes manual recording errors through a guided form-based system for logging sales and expenses, with automatic total calculations.


* Researchers: Serves as a reference model for future automated financial monitoring systems targeting small businesses in the Philippines.


* Industry (Auto Parts Sales & Services): Standardizes financial tracking across Cebu City stores, reduces common irregularities in product-level profit monitoring, and promotes data-driven practices.


* Academic Establishments: Provides BSIT students with a practical capstone model that demonstrates user-centered design, agile development methodology, and scalable PHP/MySQL web application architecture.






1.6 Definition of Terms




* Profit Lens — The proposed web-based platform for smart profit monitoring and business insights, built with PHP, MySQL, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.


* Dashboard — The main page (dashboard.php) of the system that displays real-time summaries: net profit this month, financial reports, expense tracking, and revenue insights.


* Sales Module — The module (profit.php) that allows admin users to record sales transactions by selecting a product, entering a quantity, and setting a sale date. Stock is automatically deducted upon saving.


* Expense Tracking — The module (expenses.php) that enables recording, editing, and deleting business expenses by category (e.g., Office Supplies, Advertising, Utilities, Salaries & Wages).


* Revenue Analysis — The module (revenue.php) that displays monthly revenue trends in bar and line chart formats, top-selling products, and year-over-year comparisons.












* Financial Reports — The module (reports.php) that presents a full Jan–Dec Revenue vs. Expenses grouped bar chart, a Profit & Loss table, and monthly expense breakdowns.


* Product Management — The module (products.php) that allows admin users to add, edit, and delete products with name, category, selling price, cost, and stock fields.


* Authentication — The login system (index.php) that verifies user credentials using password_verify() and PHP sessions, restricting system access to admin-role users only.


* Session — PHP's mechanism ($_SESSION) for maintaining a logged-in user's identity (user_id, email, role) across page requests without requiring re-login on each click.


* Net Profit — Calculated as Total Sales minus Total Expenses for a given period. Displayed on the dashboard, profit page, and reports module.


* formatMoney() — A helper function defined in config.php that formats numeric amounts as Philippine Peso currency (e.g., ₱15,000.00).


* Prepared Statement — A secure database query technique used throughout the system that separates SQL commands from user-supplied data, preventing SQL injection attacks.

CHAPTER 2 
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES


2.1 Theoretical Framework


​The development of Profit Lens is anchored on established theories that explain the transition of business operations from manual processes to digital automation.


​Technology Acceptance Model (TAM): This framework analyzes how end-users, specifically store owners and staff, adopt new systems. It suggests that adoption is driven by "Perceived Usefulness" (how much the tool improves work) and "Perceived Ease of Use" (how user-friendly the interface is).


​Systems Theory in Business: This theory treats a business as a collection of interconnected components. In this model, "inputs" like daily sales and expenses must be processed precisely to generate a "feedback loop"—in this case, financial reports—which is vital for the business's long-term viability. 


2.2 The Evolution of Financial Monitoring Systems


​Traditional vs. Digital Ledgers: Manual record-keeping is often prone to human error and inconsistent data summaries. Digital systems mitigate these risks through structured, form-based logging that ensures data integrity.


​Web-Based Architecture (PHP/MySQL): Modern Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) favor web platforms because they offer centralized storage and real-time data access. Implementing prepared statements in the backend ensures these platforms remain resilient against cyber threats like SQL injection.


2.3 Foreign Studies on SME Automation


​Global SME Performance: Recent international research indicates that businesses utilizing automated financial tools consistently outperform those relying on manual methods, particularly regarding accuracy and error reduction.


​Data-Driven Decision Making: Global studies show that having immediate access to revenue trends and detailed expense breakdowns empowers owners to make more confident and rapid financial choices.


​2.4 Local Studies: The Philippine SME Landscape


​The Cebu City Context: Local research focusing on auto parts retailers in Cebu emphasizes that mobile-responsive and intuitive interfaces are essential for staff members who may have limited technical expertise.


​Financial Irregularities: Research within the Philippine context suggests that manual tracking often leads to "unnoticed overspending." Profit Lens addresses this by standardizing tracking and implementing visual budget alerts. 




















CHAPTER 3


METHODOLOGY


3.1 Research Design


​This project employs a Developmental Research Design. This approach focuses on the iterative cycle of designing, building, and testing a technological solution to solve a specific, real-world operational problem for an auto parts store in Cebu City.


​3.2 System Development Methodology: The Agile Model
​The Agile Methodology was utilized for its flexibility, allowing the development team to refine features based on ongoing feedback from the stakeholder.
​Phase 1: Requirements Gathering: Identifying pain points through direct observation and interviews, specifically targeting issues like lack of summaries and overspending.
​Phase 2: Sprint Planning & System Design: Defining the MySQL database schema and creating UI wireframes for core modules like the dashboard and profit analysis pages.
​Phase 3: Development (Coding): Writing the PHP backend logic, implementing password_hash() for security, and creating helper functions for currency formatting.
​Phase 4: Testing & Iteration: Performing unit and integration tests to ensure that data entries correctly update the visual analytical charts.








3.3 System Architecture
​Profit Lens utilizes a Client-Server Architecture to ensure secure access and centralized data management.
Client Layer: The user interface built with HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript.
Server Layer: An Apache/PHP server that handles business logic, such as calculating net profit using the formula:


NetProfit = Σ(Sales Total) - Σ (Expenses Total)






Database Layer: A MySQL database (profit_lens) containing normalized tables for users, products, sales, and expenses.

​Prepared Statements: The system separates SQL commands from user inputs to neutralize SQL injection risks.

	SUPPORTING DIAGRAMS


Data Flow Diagram (DFD – Level 0)
The Level 0 DFD describes the top-level flow of data through the Profit Lens system:
* Admin User submits sales transactions (product, quantity, date) and expense records (category, amount, date) through the system's web interface.


* The Profit Lens System (PHP server) processes the submitted data: calculates totals, computes net profit (Sales Total minus Expenses Total), updates product stock quantities, and generates chart data for the dashboard and reports.


* Processed results are stored in and retrieved from the MySQL Database (profit_lens), which maintains four tables: users, products, sales, and expenses.


* The system returns generated financial reports, visual charts (bar chart, line chart, donut chart), and profit summaries back to the Admin User's browser.










Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD)
The Profit Lens database contains the following entities and relationships:


Entity
	Key Attributes
	Relationships
	users
	id (PK), email, password (hashed), role (admin/user), created_at
	An admin user records sales and expenses; only admin-role users can access system pages.
	products
	id (PK), name, category, price (selling), cost, stock, created_at
	A product is referenced in sales transactions. Stock is decremented on each sale.
	sales
	id (PK), product_id (FK), quantity, unit_price, total, sale_date, created_at
	Each sale belongs to one product. Total = unit_price x quantity. Net profit = SUM(sales.total) - SUM(expenses.amount).
	expenses
	id (PK), category, description, amount, expense_date, created_at
	Expenses are categorized (e.g., Office Supplies, Advertising, Utilities, Salaries & Wages) and aggregated for reports.
	

Foreign Key: sales.product_id references products.id (ON DELETE SET NULL — preserves historical sales if a product is deleted).
